Context

Lushe is a conceptual hair-care brand designed around the natural movement, individuality and beauty of curly hair. The project explores how a cohesive visual identity can transform everyday hair care into a more personal, expressive and enjoyable experience.

From the core brand strategy to the logo, packaging, graphic language and digital communication, the identity is built around one central idea: curls do not need to be controlled to be beautiful; they need care that understands them.

The brand story

Every curl has its own rhythm.
Hair is deeply personal. It is part of how we see ourselves, how we express ourselves and how we choose to show up in the world. Yet curly hair is often approached through routines and beauty standards that prioritise control, uniformity and manageability over natural texture. Lushe begins with a different perspective. Instead of treating curls as something to correct, it sees them as something to understand, care for and celebrate.

The brand brings together thoughtful hair care and an expressive visual identity to create a world where curls can exist in their own shape, rhythm and form.

Brand belief: Your texture is not a problem to solve. It is a part of you to celebrate.

Inspiration

The inspiration comes from the fluid, unpredictable nature of curly hair. No two curls follow exactly the same path; they twist, bend, loop and flow in their own way. This movement became the starting point for the visual identity.

Organic curves, flowing shapes and a sense of rhythm inform the logo and the broader graphic language, connecting the appearance of the brand to the very thing it is designed to care for. Visuals to show: a moodboard of natural curl patterns, flowing lines, hair textures, sculptural forms, tactile materials and colour references.

Moodboard image

The opportunity

Hair care should work with texture, not against it. Curly hair can require a different approach to cleansing, conditioning, moisture retention and styling. Finding the right routine can involve trial and error, unfamiliar product terminology and uncertainty about what works for a particular hair type.

The opportunity for Lushe is to create a brand that makes curl care feel more approachable while giving natural texture a confident, contemporary identity.

The goal is not simply to sell hair-care products, but to create an identity that makes people feel understood.

Target Audience

Designed for people who want to understand their curls.

Primary audience
Young adults aged approximately 18–30 with wavy, curly or coily hair who want products and guidance that work with their natural texture. They may be exploring curl care for the first time or refining a routine they already follow.

Secondary audience
People who have experience with textured hair care but are looking for products that fit their preferences, styling habits and lifestyle.

Audience mindset
They are not necessarily looking for perfect, identical curls every day. They want hair that feels healthy, looks like their own and fits into their lives.

Design implication: The identity should feel approachable enough for beginners, expressive enough for curl enthusiasts and sophisticated enough to stand out in a crowded beauty category.

Our brand

Lushe explores a more fluid, artistic and sculptural visual direction. Rather than relying exclusively on literal illustrations of curls, it translates the movement of hair into an abstract brand symbol and a flexible graphic language.

This gives the identity a recognisable aesthetic that can extend across bottles, labels, campaign graphics and digital touchpoints.

Campaign and digital communication

The campaign supports the brand’s core idea into communication that feels personal, expressive and consistent.

The aim is to show natural texture as an individual experience rather than a single beauty ideal. The same visual elements used on packaging carry into social media, digital touchpoints and campaign materials.

Proposed campaign concept: “#let them loose.”

The campaign celebrates the variation in natural hair textures through close-up photography, expressive typography and flowing graphic forms.
Each visual focuses on a different aspect of curl care: understanding texture, building a routine, defining curls or embracing natural movement.

Final brand system

The brand speaks with warmth, confidence and clarity. It avoids language that suggests natural curls are inherently unruly or need to be corrected.

This project explores how a brand’s visual identity can emerge from a product’s purpose rather than being added as decoration. By connecting the movement of curls to the logo and broader graphic system, the design creates a visual language that can extend across multiple touchpoints.
