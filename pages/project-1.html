Context

Dhanushkodi is one of the southernmost towns in India which was a thriving marketplace but was destroyed by a cyclone which hit in 1964 and later declared a ghost town making opportunity for a lot of stories, religious importance and myths about the place.

Concept

How can we acknowledge, witness and communicate the intangible memories of a vanished site?

Making it multisensory

The main protagonist Arun is a 41 year old Archaeologist.

He revives and witnesses some memories of his with his father who died during the cyclone.

His visits to specific places triggers memories and forces the user into thought about how the protagonist’s life might have been before all these years.

Execution

Senses we focused on for making it multisensory

Sight

Visuals of ruined Dhanushkodi and recreated scenes of the town before the cyclone help users see the contrast between past and present.

Touch

Gesture-based controls allow users to use hand movements to explore the space and reveal hidden memories, making the experience more engaging.

Hear

Ambient sounds like sea waves, wind, footsteps, conversations storm effects create an emotional and realistic atmosphere.

Smell

Sensory photographs such as sea breeze, wet sand and enhance the coastal environment and make users feel more present in the setting.

User journey

Player wears the glove
Arduino is plugged in
VR is switched on.
The player explores the place and goes to the viewpoints one by one.
The player makes the frame gesture and triggers the videos.
Memories play as a flashback
The player exits the frame and comes back to the VR world

Why chosen interactions?

  • Touch triggers memory way deeper than just looking at something. We wanted to make the user/player an active participant in the experience rather than a passive observer who just witnesses the visuals.

  • To bring a ghost town to life, we need to physically reach into its past. We trigger that on the basis of the character’s memory.

  • A glove makes it feel like your own hands are helping you connect with the past. Its also a more feasible and convenient option for making an immersive experience.

  • The glove isn’t a tech choice it’s a storytelling choice to build an emotional connection.

Experiencing the VR World

Video based storytelling

We have 4 main locations in our VR environment – Church, Stone bridge, Boats, Market so, understanding the importance of these places, we weaved a storyline around the locations which would give the player a better insight into the life of the protagonist.

The Church

Young Arun used to go to the church with his dad who was the stereotypical tamil dad with strict principles yet simple innocence. He used to ask him silly curious questions and his father answered everything.

Market

He used to whine for things to be bought which his mom denied but his father secretly bought it for him, becoming his favorite friend.

Stone bridge

Here Arun learned the seashell game and learn about philosophies running in his family from the time of his ancestors.

Boats

Arun hid among the boats and his father used to come and find him there where he taught him some principles of life which he did not forget even after he grew up.
